# Kimi K3: continuous stack-004 speed research

Speed up the **same** 25-layer, ~1.5B-parameter stack-004 model. The production objective is at least 1.5T, ideally 2T, unique training tokens across dense training and ternary QAT in 6,000 aggregate H100 GPU-hours on 32 GPUs. Research has **one H100 and a separate, cumulative three GPU-hour VM-on cap**. Keep the layer count, width, context, vocabulary, Peri-LN, value residual, optimizer mathematics, and real token count. Do not buy speed by skipping computation or silently weakening quality.

## Run the loop

Before the one authorized GPU start, have the model/data/config and a few high-value first experiments ready. After authorization, **start the VM yourself and keep running experiments continuously**. One authorization covers choosing, coding, staging, measuring, rejecting, revising and retesting ideas, plus deallocation/restart within the cap. Do not stop to ask permission between experiments. The old `research.azure_speed_worker` runs one S0 plan and shuts down; **do not use it as the continuous loop**. Use `research.speed_session` for VM lifecycle and `scripts/s0_block.py` for complete-update timing.

1. Establish a same-shape reference: one or two warmup updates, then two to five measured **complete optimizer updates** in dense and QAT. Record unique valid tokens, elapsed time, device memory, setup time, commit, and total VM-on time. Use a brief profiler capture only to locate exposed time. A CPU result is not an H100 speed result.
2. Maintain a short ordered queue of hypotheses, each with expected whole-update saving, implementation/test cost, and quality risk. The [physics sheet](docs/speed/PHYSICS.md) and [idea atlas](docs/speed/EXPERIMENTS.md) inform choices, but profiling and new ideas can change the order. Attack the dominant exposed cost, not the most fashionable kernel. Keep a running throughput/memory/correctness Pareto frontier.
3. While the VM is on, move directly from one measured experiment to the next. Write or stage the next candidate while a useful GPU test runs when possible. Do CPU coding and focused tests as needed, but do not repeatedly stop the VM for ordinary analysis. If a new candidate is not ready, use the GPU for a useful matched repeat, dense/QAT cross-check, or short profile; do not run purposeless work to claim utilization.
4. Use short matched reference/candidate screens first. Reject a clear loser quickly; repeat close/noisy results. Give longer numerical, quality and sustained-speed tests only to plausible winners. A change to numerical precision requires gradient and matched-token quality checks. Preserve failures and results in an append-only log outside Git.
5. After each result, recompute the remaining **VM-on** budget and choose the next experiment by expected production benefit per research minute. Compare continuing on the live VM with deallocating and later restarting: deallocate for a real idle interval only when its expected duration exceeds measured shutdown + restart + restaging overhead and the remaining budget still supports another useful trial. Otherwise keep the GPU on. A deallocation pauses the campaign; it does not reset the three-hour total.

Stop the research campaign only when the production token target has credible dense/QAT throughput, correctness and scaling evidence, or the three GPU-hours are spent. With only one research GPU, 32-GPU scaling remains a projection; label it as such and do not claim production success solely from a one-GPU timing. If the cap arrives before the goal, deallocate and report the best validated frontier and the unsolved bottleneck. Do not silently extend the cap. The VM cutoff timer is a fallback if the agent hangs; proactively finish and deallocate with enough time for Azure shutdown.

The first speed screen needs neither six paired minute-long runs nor a full checkpoint or production preflight. The normal production trainer keeps its preflight. The production recommendation needs dense and QAT correctness, matched-quality evidence, sustained training, and later actual 32-GPU scaling. Measure **billed VM-on time separately from timed training**; VM boot, JIT, idle thinking, uploads and shutdown all count against the three-hour research cap.

`research.speed_session` has `open`, `status`, `stage`, `exec`, and `close` actions. Its ledger must live outside Git, results must stay on a persistent disk, and `open` is a paid action: invoke it only after the one-time campaign authorization. Use `close --reason goal` when the evidence meets the goal, `close --reason budget` at the cap, or `close --reason idle` only for a cost-justified pause. The [README](docs/speed/README.md) shows the commands. Run continuously until one of those terminal conditions; an agent conversation ending is not a valid reason to leave the VM billed.
