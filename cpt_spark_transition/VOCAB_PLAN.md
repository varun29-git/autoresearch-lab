# Vocabulary plan: cut unused scripts, keep learned Indic

Date: 2026-11-13. Status: the cut is specified and the CPU report is reproducible. No checkpoint weights are written. Nothing outside this folder is edited.

This is the vocabulary step in front of [SOTA_PLAN.md](SOTA_PLAN.md). If this plan is accepted, the learning-rate screen starts from the post-surgery checkpoint. Research: [research/vocab_surgery.md](research/vocab_surgery.md). The measured keep and drop counts are [research/closure_report.json](research/closure_report.json), regenerated by `python -m vocab.report`.

The seven languages are Hindi, Marathi, Kannada, Telugu, Tamil, Gujarati, and Bengali, plus English. Scripts that stay: Latin, digits, punctuation, symbols, Greek, emoji, and Devanagari, Bengali, Gujarati, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada.

## 1. What is cut, and what is not

Cutting is required. The tied embedding is `[vocab, 2048]`. At 131,072 rows it is 268,435,456 parameters, 512 MiB in bf16, and it is also the softmax. A script the device will never emit still costs a row and a logit.

The rows that are cut are the ones outside English, code, math, and the seven languages: CJK, kana, hangul, Cyrillic, Arabic, Hebrew, and the Indic scripts that are not targets (Gurmukhi, Odia, Malayalam, Sinhala), plus the other non-target scripts listed in `vocab/model.py`. A piece is removed only when its decoded characters meet that drop set and it is not a merge ancestor of a piece that stays. The byte alphabet, Spark’s control tokens at ids 0–13, and the unused tail stay.

Spark’s own pieces for the seven target scripts stay. Probe 4 counted 3,840 of them, and their row norms show they were trained, not left at initialization. Deleting them would throw away the Indic behavior this checkpoint already has. Sarvam does not replace those rows. A Sarvam piece whose surface is already a Spark piece reuses that row and its exact vector. Only a Sarvam piece Spark does not already contain is appended.

Malayalam, Odia, Gurmukhi, and the other non-target scripts are not given Sarvam pieces. Bengali-script Assamese would take the Bengali path. That is not Assamese support. Romanized Hindi stays on the Spark path.

The cut is not a round number. Spark already has 64,602 ASCII pieces. A 65,536 cap cannot hold those pieces and the Sarvam additions. Gemma-Prune’s mobile pipeline collapsed English when it kept dictionary words and dropped the real BPE merges. Probe 8’s script-range delete left English identical on 97% of 300 lines. This cut refuses that. Merge ancestors are restored before a merge is dropped, including the rare intermediate that exists only to build a common English word.

Measured on the stock Base tokenizer (`sha256 710cce15cf3565674c499f9413997c6e8101f2bdd96245cff8f0311fb501248c`):

- 77,842 Spark rows kept, 53,230 dropped. No kept row decodes to a dropped script, so the ancestor rule did not have to rescue a CJK piece.
- 3,357 Sarvam pieces already exist as Spark rows. They keep those ids and those vectors.
- 36,434 Sarvam pieces are new. The finished vocabulary is 114,276.
- Spark contains 243 of the 256 byte symbols. The missing 13 are invalid UTF-8 bytes it never allocated. Every symbol it does have is kept.
- On the fixed samples, tokens per word fell in all seven languages. The Telugu sentence went from 5.17 to 2.33. English, code, and math piece strings matched stock Spark, including indentation, accents, Greek, and the literal specials.

That is a tokenizer measurement. It is not a loss measurement.

## 2. The encoder

A document with no character in the seven scripts is encoded by Spark’s own BPE, with the dropped merges removed. Piece strings match the stock tokenizer. Ids 0–13 stay. Every other surviving Spark id is packed in original order after them.

A document that does contain those scripts is routed. Protected whitespace and Latin spans use the kept Spark merges. Indic spans use the Sarvam score table: NFC, a leading U+0120, ordinary spaces become U+0120, highest score merges first, equal scores merge the leftmost pair. The two merge lists are never concatenated. Donor specials and the twelve reserved placeholders are not added. The 25,189-piece English tail in the 65,536-piece file is not used.

The donor file is `tokenizer/tokenizer-65k.json`, sha256 `c53e0e95cd19e2954a8c7114c0a580eeb16698b153fdf84c5d80a7187d778d29`. The stock Spark tokenizer used for the report is `XHToken/Spark-X2.6-1.7B-Base` `tokenizer.json`, sha256 `710cce15cf3565674c499f9413997c6e8101f2bdd96245cff8f0311fb501248c`, kept outside git.

## 3. How new rows are born

The tied matrix stays one parameter. Kept rows are copied bit for bit from the original embedding, in the packed order. The backbone is not touched.

A new piece whose surface is `s` (U+0120 written as a normal space) is initialized from the stock Spark tokenization of `s`:

```text
rows = original_embedding[stock_spark.encode(s)]
e    = mean(rows)
e    = e * (mean(norm(rows)) / norm(e))
```

The mean is FVT. The scale puts the norm back on the mean norm of those constituents. The constituent ids are the original Spark ids, so an existing Indic piece that Spark uses to spell `s` contributes its learned vector. The new row is the only place that vector is averaged. The old row itself is still in the matrix, unchanged.

No real checkpoint is written by this folder. `vocab/transplant.py` is the function that will build the matrix when a weight tensor is supplied. The tests run it on a synthetic tensor.

## 4. Alignment

Alignment trains only the appended rows. Every Spark row, including the seven-script rows, is frozen.

On English, code, and math, the teacher is the original model with the deleted rows removed and the softmax recomputed over the kept rows. That is the distribution of the cut before any new row exists. The student matches it with KL divergence. The teacher’s probability on each new id is zero, so a new row that fires on English is penalized. The implementation is `english_kl` in `vocab/alignment.py`.

On the seven languages, the loss is next-token cross-entropy under this encoder. Where the encoder emitted an old Spark id, the target is that id, so the update still lands only on new rows and the old Indic classifier is not the thing being trained. Where the encoder emitted a new id, the new row learns to be the class. `indic_cross_entropy` is that loss.

After every optimizer step the kept rows are copied back from the snapshot, including the fp32 master. A zero gradient does not save a row under AdamW decay. `restore_kept_rows` does the copy. The tests run one decaying AdamW step and check the kept rows against the snapshot.

Alignment stops at the first of: English held-out negative log-likelihood within the bf16 double-run floor of the original model, Indic held-out negative log-likelihood flattening, or 1B scored tokens for a person to review. Its learning rate is its own short sweep. It is not the CPT screen of 1e-5, 3e-5, and 1e-4. Full CPT then starts from this checkpoint. No GPU job is launched from this work.

## 5. Gates

The CPU report and `tests/test_vocab_cut.py` cover the gates that do not need weights:

- Piece-string identity against stock Spark on English, code, and math with none of the seven scripts, including indentation, repeated spaces, accents, Greek, and the literal specials.
- Ids 0–13 unchanged. Every kept merge’s two inputs are kept. All 256 byte symbols are kept.
- `नमस्ते`, `हिन्दी`, and `ಕನ್ನಡ` match the donor piece strings. The mixed line keeps Spark’s tokenization of `main` and `hoon` and the donor’s pieces for `जा रहा`.
- A known trained Devanagari row is still in the map. A known CJK row is not.
- Indic token counts on the fixed samples are lower than stock Spark.

These still wait for a real forward pass, and they are not claimed here: the tie after `save_pretrained`, the English loss delta beside a bf16 double run, and the one-step optimizer check on the real embedding. The optimizer check on a synthetic tensor is in the tests.

## 6. What this refuses

- Deleting a Spark piece of the seven target scripts because a Sarvam piece can spell the same text.
- Rebuilding one BPE by concatenating Sarvam merges onto Spark’s merge table.
- Replacing Spark’s English encoder with the 65,536-piece English tail.
- Frequency-pruning inside the English merge closure to hit 65,536 or 96,000.
- `mean_resizing`, random new rows, or a Gaussian around the old mean.
- Untying the embedding.
- Editing `terminus/`, `tokenizer/`, `production/`, or `Alternate direction/`.
