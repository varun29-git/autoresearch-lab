# Terminus hyperparameter autoresearch: campaign design

**Planning date:** 2026-10-08. **Status:** proposal; no GPU campaign authorized or started. This is a new, versioned campaign after `architecture-20261006c`. The architecture incumbent is the 25-layer Peri-LN plus learned value-residual stack. The stage-one English result (2.65363 versus 2.68445 dense loss at 2.5B tokens; 2.81095 versus 2.82651 after a matched 500M-token QAT probe) motivates tuning it, but does not establish a 1.5B multilingual recipe. See the [final architecture report](../notes/FINAL_REPORT_ARCHITECTURE_20261006.md). This plan does not change that campaign's lock, scorer, training code, or published receipts.

**2026-10-10 update:** the next tuning work is dense-only on the fixed 25-layer stack, retaining the completed speed optimizations. The [Muon scaling decision](MUON_SCALING_DECISION_20261010.md) supersedes the earlier hidden-Muon width-rule proposal and gives the minimal next comparisons. The geometry, QAT, broad sweep, and initial kernel-gate sections below preserve the earlier planning context; they are not a mandatory queue for this next search. No optimizer change or new paid campaign is implemented by this update.

## 1. Decision and scope

Find a **reproducible dense-FP8 → ternary-weight/FP8-activation training recipe** for the selected architecture, with measured quality, stability, and GPU cost. The search covers Muon and auxiliary AdamW rates, Muon width scaling, weight decay, momentum/betas, effective batch, clipping, WSD warmup/stable/decay, dense/QAT allocation, QAT re-warmup and optimizer-state policy. Data mixture and quantizer are important neighboring axes, but change them only in separately versioned subcampaigns with matched controls; otherwise optimizer effects become uninterpretable. Tokenizer, architecture mechanisms, and final evaluation definitions are frozen within each subcampaign. Keep the value-residual and Peri-LN mechanisms at all widths.

The primary selection target is **final ternary quality** at a declared total training compute budget. Report dense quality, ternary quality, per-domain/task quality, tokens-to-quality, sustained GPU-hours, memory, and incidents as separate axes. Use English for inexpensive diagnosis; a recipe cannot graduate to production on English alone. A lower proxy loss is evidence, not a production verdict. The selected *mechanisms* are Peri-LN and value residual; the 25-layer, 2304-wide target geometry is still a design choice to confirm. If the owner declares 25 layers fixed, its geometry check becomes a documented limitation rather than a prerequisite for µP.

**Boundary:** the old `program.md` and `research/policy.json` govern the completed architecture campaign. First finish the [pre-HPO kernel gate](PRE_HPO_KERNEL_GATE.md), which freezes either a certified faster implementation or the current tuned baseline; the initial combined fusion prototype measured slower at 1.5B and must not be assumed beneficial. Then create a separate `hpo/v1` policy, scorer, campaign lock, program, and trial runner using the existing archive/ledger/Azure-cycle machinery. Once `hpo/v1` starts, these controls are immutable. Changes to optimizer, model parameterization, precision, QAT transition, or scoring are new explicit recipe/campaign versions, never silent edits to a running yardstick. The human launches and stops paid GPU work.

## 2. Evidence behind the design

Primary papers and the Microsoft README were checked on 2026-10-08. Published results are priors; the Terminus measurements below are separate evidence.

The [public optimizer-group implementation review](HPO_OPTIMIZER_GROUPS_RESEARCH.md) separately checks Moonlight, nanochat, modded-nanogpt, and Microsoft's `mup` code. Successful recipes range from a shared base LR with matrix-shape correction to explicit embedding/head/scalar/Muon roles with separate rates and optimizer settings. Their raw LR values are not portable to our tied embedding and pinned Muon convention. The [optimizer-tuning research protocol](OPTIMIZER_TUNING_RESEARCH.md) adds a separately priced, **tuned all-AdamW control**, physical-role search, decay-product accounting, and the relevant transfer limits. Its four-point all-AdamW matrix screen adds **4B proxy tokens minimum including one full continuation**; the 23 coarse branches below count only the original hybrid Muon menu and do not include that control. The [concrete single-GPU test matrix for eventual 32-GPU production](HPO_TEST_MATRIX_32GPU.md) is the more specific queue when 25 layers is fixed and a production batch up to 2.1M tokens is being considered; its additional completed LR neighbors and batch gate have separately priced volumes. Single-GPU accumulation emulates that batch now; 32-rank checks wait for the production cluster.

Local counterevidence matters. The archived 150M dense run used peak norm LR 0.01 and developed a layer-0 attention-gain collapse and huge residual drift; the architecture campaign used norm LR 2e-4 and the selected stack bounded residuals. In the archived 10B→2B QAT run, one-shot conversion caused a large loss spike and QAT remained worse than dense on every held-out source. The selected stack's 500M QAT probe had a *larger immediate shock* than baseline but recovered to a better final loss. Thus early conversion loss, late QAT loss, and functional scale must all be observed.

### LR-range audit before spending

The [completed architecture campaign](../notes/FINAL_REPORT_ARCHITECTURE_20261006.md) used **one optimizer recipe**, not an LR sweep: Muon base 0.003, tied embedding AdamW 0.003, main/Peri/final norm 0.0002, and Q/K gain 0.0001. It establishes that recipe's stability and quality at its tested horizon; it does **not** establish that any rate is optimal. The [older Lean 150M run](../experiments/terminus_lean_150m_qat_2b/analysis/COMBINED_DENSE_QAT_SCALING_REVIEW.md) completed 10B dense tokens without nonfinite incidents at Muon **0.01**, embedding **0.01**, and norm **0.01** simultaneously. Its attention-gain collapse and residual drift prevent calling that recipe good, and the coupled changes prevent assigning the pathology to one LR. They do show that 0.006 is not a defensible *a priori* ceiling for Muon and that embedding 0.003 needs a higher-side test with norm held low. The old `terminus.search_plan` grid included 0.009 but was a dry-run proposal, not measured evidence.

The [pinned optimizer](../terminus/optim.py) sets each Muon group's peak LR to `base_lr × 0.2√fan_in`; upstream Muon additionally applies `√max(1, rows/columns)` to the orthogonalized update. For a width-768 input projection the actual group LR is **0.00831 / 0.01663 / 0.03326 / 0.06651** at base **0.0015 / 0.003 / 0.006 / 0.012**, respectively. The archived width-640 run's base 0.01 meant a group LR of **0.05060** for its input projections. These are optimizer implementation checks, **not** comparable AdamW elementwise LRs or evidence of the best loss. The larger 0.012 point deliberately tests beyond the previous stable-control region while separate auxiliary groups stay fixed. No paper supplies a trustworthy absolute Terminus peak LR; the validated conclusion is that the old bracket could miss a higher optimum, and only matched training can locate it.

Decay-convention nuance: [Practical Efficiency of Muon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02222), Eq. 1, calls its matrix decay “coupled,” but writes `W_new = W − η[(0.2√n)O + λW]`. The pinned Terminus group applies `LR=ηs`, `WD=λ/s` and then `W_new=(1−LR×WD)W−LR×O`, which has the same algebraic `ηλ` matrix shrinkage when the update scale `s` and schedule align. Different matrix normalization, optimizer implementation, and parameterization still prevent copying the paper's optimum. Group-specific LR overrides in the HPO builder leave physical WD fixed and therefore change the decay product; the proposed campaign records this and tests matched-shrinkage counterfactuals when it matters.

## 3. Parameterization is a versioned experiment, not a label

The present optimizer has two unproven width rules: `sqrt_fan_in` and `sqrt_fan_in_ratio`. Both start from `0.2 sqrt(fan_in)` and the latter adds `fan_in_base/fan_in`; both divide matrix weight decay by their LR scale so the scheduled per-step decay product stays unchanged. The current `transfer_diag.py` correctly calls its report *empirical Muon width transfer*, not µP. Switching that rule alone does not implement µP.

The [32-GPU scale-transfer note](OPTIMIZER_SCALE_TRANSFER_32GPU.md) spells out which width predictions remain conditional, why an approximately 100M proxy would alter this scale family's geometry, and how global batch arithmetic changes on 32 data-parallel GPUs. Define a complete `parameterization_id` with a checked role table before any HPO training. For each row specify stored weight shape, effective forward multiplier, initialization variance, optimizer, peak LR as a function of width, decay convention, and conversion to the ternary effective weight. Required rows:

1. Tied input embedding and readout, including separate accounting of lookup and softmax gradients and the effective readout multiplier. Do not apply an extra readout factor to a trained checkpoint without a validated function-preserving transformation.
2. Logical Q, K, V slices inside fused QKV; O; logical gate and up inside fused gate/up; down. Muon orthogonalizes logical slices, so both shape correction and diagnostic rates are **per slice**, even if they share one physical parameter/state tensor. A role-specific rate needs explicit tested slice-aware update semantics.
3. Main, Peri-LN output, final, and learned Q/K normalization gains; learned value-residual scalars; any other trainable scalar/vector. These use auxiliary AdamW unless separately justified.
4. The fixed 128-dimensional attention head, three-query-per-KV GQA mapping, tied 65K vocabulary, and FP8/ternary effective-weight path. Width transfer varies `d_model`, Q/KV head count, and FFN proportion together while holding head dimension, depth 25, attention pattern, context, tokenizer, data, and total training horizon fixed for that test. Proposed aligned bridge: widths 768/1536/2304 with 6/12/18 query heads, 2/4/6 KV heads, and 1920/3840/5760 FFN dimensions. Verify parameter counts and memory before locking.

The `hpo/v1` recipe schema should name main block norms, Peri output norms, final norm, Q/K gains, and learned value-residual `value_lambda` **separately**, even when their initial LR/betas/decay equal the completed architecture control. The current optimizer places all non-Q/K vectors and scalars in one AdamW group, obscuring per-role accounting and preventing independent rate selection for these roles. Partitioning with identical update settings needs exact optimizer-step and resume-equivalence tests before opening the campaign. This role split is not permission to assign separate input and readout LRs to the tied embedding; that is one parameter. Conditional role-specific LR branches follow measured sensitivity, avoiding a large Cartesian grid.

**Implementation checkpoint:** [`build_hpo_optimizer`](../terminus/hpo_optimizer.py) now provides this explicit role partition and optional per-role rates as an opt-in HPO builder. It wraps the archived optimizer builder and leaves architecture-campaign training behavior untouched. Muon role overrides are **base** rates before shape scaling; auxiliary overrides are direct AdamW rates. Other optimizer settings remain unchanged, so a rate override also changes `LR × weight_decay` where decay is nonzero; log that shrinkage in each trial and tune WD separately. CPU tests check group coverage, baseline-step parity, role override validation, and resume parity; an isolated checkout of `trial/stack/004` confirmed its actual selected-stack parameter names and exact one-step CPU parity. The group layout is different from architecture-campaign checkpoints, so HPO starts fresh or needs an explicitly tested state migration. The HPO campaign runner/manifest integration and native H100 parity are still unimplemented/unrun.

Build **two coherent arms**: current Terminus parameterization/Muon rule as a control and an explicitly derived µP-compatible arm. A third empirical Muon width correction is warranted only if coordinate/transfer diagnostics show the first two fail. The µP arm must state how tied readout scaling and embedding LR interact, how Q/K gain changes affect logits, and how Peri-LN/value-residual scalars scale. At the base width, preserve the original function at initialization where possible; if the wider arm changes effective weights, train it from scratch and compare its *absolute quality* as well as transfer consistency. Do not assume the Microsoft `MuAdam` optimizer implements Muon.

An implementable **starting derivation**, subject to the numerical gate, is the tied-weight form in Tensor Programs V Table 8. Let `d0=768`, `r=d/d0`, and store one embedding/readout matrix `E` initialized with variance `1/d0` at **every** width. Use `Embedding(x,E)` for input and `Linear(h,E)/r` for logits, so the base-width forward function matches the existing readout multiplier. Give this vector-like matrix a width-constant auxiliary AdamW base LR. For hidden projection matrices initialized with variance proportional to `1/fan_in`, use the [reviewed Muon rule and author implementation](MUON_SCALING_DECISION_20261010.md#1-what-is-already-well-supported): the coefficient on raw NS output is `a_j(base) × sqrt[(fan_out/fan_out_base)/(fan_in/fan_in_base)]`. It stays constant for each proportionally widened logical matrix. Relative to our current combined Moonshot coefficient, the correction is `sqrt(fan_in_base/fan_in)`, with a separate base shape for each logical role. The old extra `fan_in_base/fan_in` rule overcorrects in this derivation and is only a historical empirical counterfactual. Adam's hidden LR exponent is not Muon's exponent. Keep scalar value-residual rates width-constant as a first hypothesis; test norm gains and Q/K gains separately. The current model uses `E` initialization variance `1/d` and no readout multiplier, so the two arms differ genuinely at widths above 768. Exact initialization, decoupled-decay, Q/K scale, and FP8 mapping equations belong in the checked role table before implementation; ternary mapping is later work. Head dimension stays 128: changing the usual `1/sqrt(128)` attention scale to `1/128` would be a constant change, not a width correction in this bridge, and needs its own multiplier/control if tried.

**Numerical gate:** run fixed real-token mini-batches at all three widths for initialization and after 1, 10, and at least 100 optimizer updates, under both reference BF16 and intended FP8 where affordable. Record per-role parameter/gradient/update RMS, spectral norm or stable approximation, update-to-weight and update-to-activation ratios, residual/branch RMS by layer, Q/K logit RMS and entropy, tied input/readout contributions, final logit distribution, FP8 scale/overflow/zero rates, and loss. A coordinate curve must neither systematically blow up nor vanish with width for relevant activations and logits. Compare **LR ranking** at 768 and 1536; only then claim local transfer, and verify one near-optimum and one bracket point at 2304. No finite diagnostic proves a µP theorem for this exact architecture.

### Geometry confirmation and the limits of width µP

**Layer count is an architecture choice, not a parameter that µP optimizes.** Original µP chiefly gives a width prescription for a fixed computation graph. Tensor Programs V reported some empirical depth transfer with caveats; newer Complete-P/Complete(d)-P work proposes depth rules, but those results do not validate Terminus's Muon, Peri-LN, value residual, FP8, or ternary path. If the target is allowed to move from 25 layers, run a small, separately locked `geometry/v1` confirmation **before expensive target-width HPO**. Retain the 25-layer stack as control and test feasible 20/25/30-layer stacks (multiples of the current five-layer attention cycle). Preserve Peri-LN/value residual, tokenizer, context, data, precision, and evaluation. Recompute the depth-dependent residual initialization (currently approximately `1/sqrt(2L)`), and check initial branch/residual scale and value-residual behavior rather than copying the 25-layer constant. Test two complementary comparisons: (a) fixed width/FFN ratio, reporting the extra parameters and FLOPs; (b) approximately matched parameters by reallocating FFN width or model width, reporting the changed shapes. The latter is a depth-versus-capacity-allocation comparison, not a pure depth ablation. Price and compare equal-token *and* equal-GPU-hour outcomes, memory, and short QAT sensitivity. Give every geometry a local Muon/auxiliary LR bracket so one shape is not rejected solely because 25 layers' optimum transferred poorly. Promotion requires a completed decay and a clear quality/cost or domain benefit; short screens only reject obvious failures. A changed winner receives a new scale-family and parameterization ID, fresh width diagnostics, and local HPO before production confirmation.

Other boundaries are equally explicit. Width µP does not decide head dimension or GQA repetition, FFN expansion, local/global attention pattern or context length, normalization placement, residual scaling with **depth**, tokenizer/vocabulary, data mixture, weight decay as regularization, batch size, token horizon, or ternary quantizer/STE. The tied readout, learned Q/K and Peri-LN gains, value-residual scalars, logical Muon slices, FP8 scales, and QAT update effects need the role and coordinate checks above even when only width changes. Change one of the structural/numerical boundaries in a versioned study, then recheck the relevant LR/batch neighborhood. A targeted check follows a measured failure or a credible quality/cost hypothesis; a full Cartesian architecture search is not required to use µP.

Do not stop merely because a gradient was clipped. ### Exact first proxy queue if depth is open

The first committed `geometry/v1` queue is **12 dense branches**: `G20-M15/G20-M30/G20-M60/G20-M120`, `G25-M15/G25-M30/G25-M60/G25-M120`, and `G30-M15/G30-M30/G30-M60/G30-M120` (run and report each depth's rates in ascending order). `G` is layer count; `M15/M30/M60/M120` means Muon **base** peak LR 0.0015/0.003/0.006/0.012. All retain width 768, 6 query/2 KV heads and the Peri-LN plus value-residual stack. To keep parameter count near 200M while respecting 128 alignment, the proposed FFN widths are **2560/1920/1536** for 20/25/30 layers respectively (calculated counts approximately **199.82M/200.33M/203.79M**, to verify with `count_parameters` before locking). This is explicitly a **depth-versus-FFN-capacity** comparison. Recompute `residual_init_scale≈1/sqrt(2L)` at each depth. Use the current `sqrt_fan_in` Muon rule for this geometry comparison; the µP arm is tested after a geometry family is chosen. These twelve branches are separate from the later width-transfer check. If depth is fixed at 25, omit the 20- and 30-layer branches and keep the **4 Muon-LR** branches.

Every geometry branch uses the same frozen English stream, tokenizer and held-out blocks, seed 1337, 4,096 context, 1,024 local window, global attention every fifth layer, head dimension 128, 3:1 Q:KV ratio, dense FP8, **393,216 tokens/update** (`32×3×4096` on one GPU), embedding/readout AdamW LR **0.003**, main/Peri/final norm LR **0.0002**, Q/K gain LR **0.0001**, matrix/embedding WD **0.1**, norm WD **0**, Muon momentum **0.95**, AdamW betas **(0.9,0.95)**, ε **1e-10**, and gradient clip **1.0**. Each has the *same 2.5B-token WSD horizon*: **250M warmup, 1.75B stable, 500M cosine decay, final LR fraction 0.1**. The **0.5B-token** screen is a checkpoint of that schedule. These controls reproduce the architecture-stage optimizer region while varying only depth/shape and Muon base LR.

At the previous 200M stack median 143.5K tokens/s, 12B dense is about **23.2 active H100 hours** before startup/validation differences; QAT and dense continuations add their measured costs.

### Subsequent HPO branches at the selected depth

Use the winning geometry and a new `hpo/v1` lock. Each optimizer/batch **coarse branch runs 0.5B tokens on a declared 2.5B WSD schedule**; its winner and any unresolved near tie continue to **2.5B total**. Change one row at a time around the current incumbent, retaining the incumbent as a reusable control only when its source, data, schedule, seed, and scorer hashes match exactly. The ordered rows are: (1) embedding LR **0.0003/0.001/0.003/0.01**; (2) Muon LR local recheck **η/2, η, 2η** after changing embedding policy; (3) main/Peri norm LR **0.0001/0.0002/0.0004/0.0008** and then Q/K LR **0.00005/0.0001/0.0002/0.0004**; (4) WD **0/0.03/0.1/0.3**, then momentum **0.90/0.95/0.98** only if the LR/WD region is stable; (5) effective batch **131,072/262,144/393,216/524,288**, with **η/2,η,2η** rechecked on the best two batches. With one incumbent point reused per row, this menu contains **up to 23 additional 0.5B-token coarse branches** if every row is funded: 3 embedding + 2 Muon recheck + 3 norm + 3 Q/K + 3 WD + 2 momentum + 3 batch + 4 batch/LR interactions. Boundary expansions, near-tie continuations, and repeated seeds are priced separately. Betas, ε, clipping, and individual Q/K/O/down multipliers are **conditional diagnostic branches**, not mandatory first-wave trials. The actual branch count after the first twelve is adaptive because only surviving rows are funded; every added branch has a declared token budget and ID before launch.

Schedule branches are different: after choosing an optimizer/batch region, test warmup **5/10/15%**, decay **10/20/30%**, and final fraction **0.01/0.1** one axis at a time, each to a **completed 2.5B-token dense horizon**. With the 10/20/0.1 incumbent reused, that is **up to 5 new full-horizon branches** (2 warmup + 2 decay + 1 final fraction). The 0.5B checkpoint cannot compare decay choices because none has reached decay. If all optional rows are funded, the 23 coarse optimizer/batch branches alone use **11.5B tokens**, and five schedule branches use **12.5B more**, before any continuation, width bridge, or QAT. They are successive budget gates, not a commitment to launch every branch. For QAT, branch from the same completed dense parent with peak multipliers **0.25/0.5/1.0**, using **0.5B QAT** for shock/recovery screens; fully decay promising branches to **2B QAT** alongside a 2B same-data dense continuation. Test QAT re-warmup, decay, final fraction, and carry/reset policy sequentially only after the peak-rate region is known. Width-transfer checks use the same selected base rates at 768/1536/2304, with current versus derived µP scaling and a small sentinel LR bracket; the 768-width runs are reused when both parameterizations are functionally identical at the base width.

Keep architecture and tokenizer fixed during optimizer attribution. After a stable optimizer/width rule exists, open separately locked comparisons for data mixture, quantizer/ternary group size, precision, or objective, then recheck the leading LR/batch region because interactions can move the optimum. The former `terminus.search_plan` jobs and 60-hour dry run were drafted for an earlier architecture and assumed 200M/700M throughput; use them as examples of ledger shape, not as a ready HPO budget or runnable policy.

## 5. Autoresearch loop and experiment design

1. **Prepare and lock.** Complete [K0–K5](PRE_HPO_KERNEL_GATE.md) on the selected stack before optimizer comparisons, including matched profiling, isolated candidate timing, numerical checks, and a frozen runtime choice. Then freeze the architecture commit and a scale-family spec, dataset/held-out/task hashes, scorer, search policy, source/runtime pins, spend ceiling, trial limit, and protected code paths. Real data availability and all-language/task coverage are checked first. Untouched final tasks remain outside the loop. Reprice 1.5B and proxy training from the new all-in memory/throughput receipts. If target geometry is open, run `geometry/v1` under its own lock after coarse optimizer/parameterization checks, then lock HPO to its chosen family.
2. **Validate numerical semantics.** Unit/reference tests for each parameterization and optimizer role: fused versus unfused logical Muon; exact per-role LR/decay schedule; masked accumulation; FP8 forward/backward parity; native SM90; checkpoint/resume; dense→QAT state/RNG/cursor continuity. Test a parameterization before judging its loss. A kernel optimization has a separate parity gate.
3. **Broad proxy sweep.** At width 768, use a small, predeclared fractional/local design: Muon LR × embedding LR first, then auxiliary norm/QK rates, then WD/momentum, then batch×local LR, then schedule. Use the same data order and evaluation blocks for paired arms. Seed replication is reserved for close candidates; do not spend all compute on a single lucky seed.
4. **Telescoping width bridge (new method worth embedding).** Around the 768 optimum, run a smaller local mesh at 1536; measure whether the optimum drifts and whether the 2304 bracket contains it. Narrow the mesh only when the lower-width optimum is interior and curvature/uncertainty make the narrowed region credible; keep sentinel points outside it. The published telescoping study used 8×8→5×5→3×3→1×1 across widths; our expensive, multi-axis search should use adaptive local meshes instead of copying that grid. Record both finite-width drift and mesh uncertainty. A failed bridge sends the role/rule back to derivation or direct target-width tuning.
5. **Horizon bridge.** For a chosen width and batch, compare rate rankings at two **fully decayed**, predeclared horizons plus a small target-horizon bracket. A screen checkpoint on a longer WSD schedule can reject failures, but its stable-phase loss is not the final-horizon objective. Fit an LR-versus-horizon relation only if there are enough distinct horizons and a visible interior optimum; otherwise keep a conservative bracket. Never call width transfer proof of duration transfer.
6. **QAT branch search.** Use exact trained dense parents at declared switch points; each QAT variant branches from a common parent and a common fresh continuation stream. At each switch compare immediate zero-update ternary loss, early recovery, final decayed ternary loss, and the same-cost dense continuation. Compare earlier versus later switch at matched **total tokens and GPU-hours**, counting their different parent histories. Test current blockwise quantizer first; alternate quantizer is a separate campaign version with its own correctness gates.
7. **Confirmation.** Repeat near ties with at least two seeds and verify promising 1536/2304 recipes on the frozen multilingual development suite. Require an actual target-width run through decay and QAT before production selection. Preserve a Pareto shortlist if a quality/cost/domain tradeoff remains. Report sensitivity bands, not only the best point.

The agent chooses the next trial from diagnosed uncertainty and published evidence, writes a pre-trial hypothesis and falsifier, commits the complete recipe, receives a trial ID, and reads all downloaded logs only after verified VM shutdown. Failures remain ledger entries. Do not mutate a candidate commit or reuse an ID. No automatic loop may launch or stop paid GPU resources without the owner's explicit campaign launch.

## 6. Scores, promotion, and guardrails

- **Matched comparisons:** same tokenizer, validation examples, task definitions, source mixture, sequence policy, and data order where feasible. At fixed tokenizer compare equal tokens; for cost selection compare equal measured GPU-hours too. At different effective batches report both equal-token and equal-update views, since neither alone identifies all effects. Validation uncertainty uses document-aware paired resampling plus independent seeds for finalists; the confidence interval is descriptive after adaptive search, not a free significance guarantee.
- **Quality:** per-source next-token loss and bits/byte where useful, fixed-weight aggregate, learning-curve area, answer-only task loss/margins and available accuracy. Mark missing task coverage. No final test-set peeking. Dense and QAT scores are distinct; conversion shock is diagnostic, not the terminal score.
- **Numerics and function:** nonfinite incidents are disqualifying until fixed in a new version. Track clipping and actual update-to-activation, Peri-LN output and residual RMS by layer, value-residual coefficients, Q/K gain/logit/entropy, embedding/readout logits, FP8 errors, ternary reconstruction and layerwise function error. Compare each signal to paired baseline and functional performance; avoid universal thresholds for healthy attention or residual size.
- **Systems:** all-in sustained tokens/s, update time breakdown, GPU duty/power, peak allocated/reserved memory, checkpoint/validation overhead, parameter and optimizer-state bytes, QAT conversion overhead, cloud billing and verified artifact size. A faster step can require more tokens to reach quality.
- **Promotion:** early screen rejects only crashes, obvious regressions, or dominated candidates with a credible margin. A candidate needs completed decay and replicated or bridged evidence before it becomes incumbent. A transfer claim requires an interior LR optimum at the proxy and an overlapping near-optimal region at the bridge/target; otherwise label it empirical failure or unresolved. Keep the published architecture control alongside any new parameterization so a stable transfer rule cannot win by making every width worse.

## 7. Harness and receipts to build before running

Implement a new `hpo/` package or sibling campaign module while reusing `research.azure_cycle` archiving and `terminus` training. The new campaign must accept **immutable recipe manifests** rather than agent edits to protected `train.py`. A manifest names architecture/source hash, `parameterization_id`, scale-family row, optimizer groups and logical slices, phase schedule, batch decomposition, seed, data scope, eval hash, runtime pins, and parent checkpoint when applicable. It resolves every rate and decay multiplier at each width and phase into a canonical JSON and hash. The runner rejects unknown keys, invalid combinations, out-of-budget jobs, dirty source, altered data/eval, missing native preflight, non-resumable parent, and a published trial ID with different content.

Each trial writes an append-only row with proposed mechanism, predicted signal, exact commit/config/data hashes, stage, seed, parent checkpoint, requested and realized tokens/updates, schedule phases, per-role effective LR and cumulative decay, loss/task/stability trajectories, measured GPU-hours, failure class, artifact receipt hashes, and a review decision. Keep checkpoints, shard manifests containing private paths, raw evaluation records, generated trial results, and credentials **outside Git**. Small review notes and code commits remain in Git. A status command should print the full score vector, phase boundaries, per-role diagnostics, and Pareto frontier, with links to external receipts.

Local acceptance tests: schema rejection, role coverage and fused-slice accounting, width scaling at all three shapes, schedule/decay integration, exact batch arithmetic and partial updates, paired scorer, trial immutability, resume equivalence, QAT conversion cursor/state mapping, and budget accounting. On the H100, require real-token native parity and short training/resume receipts before any sweep. The current local host cannot establish SM90 parity; record it as unrun until the owner launches a GPU validation session.

## 8. Budget and launch gate

### Proposed token ladder

Token counts below are **per trial/recipe**, cumulative within a training stage, and are proposed campaign settings. A checkpoint at 0.5B on a 2.5B WSD schedule is not a separately decayed 0.5B run. The same applies to a 2B checkpoint of a 10B schedule. Final promotion needs a completed decay. The current English architecture view has only 5.748B prepared training tokens, so longer fresh-data studies require a new prepared, versioned manifest; do not silently repeat that view.

| Question | Token exposure | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| µP/optimizer coordinate and native numerical check | 1, 10, then 100 updates per configuration; at the 393,216-token proxy batch, 100 updates = **39.3M tokens** | Checks scales, parity, memory, and incidents. No quality ranking. |
| 20/25/30-layer geometry at ~200M parameters | **4 Muon LRs per shape × 0.5B dense**; continue at least one LR per depth to **2.5B dense** | Twelve initial screens; compares depth/capacity only after full decay. See the exact queue above. |
| 200M Muon/auxiliary HPO | **0.5B dense** per coarse recipe; **2.5B dense** for shortlisted recipes | Uses the same exposure as the completed architecture campaign, with a new HPO lock and fresh runs where parameterization changes. |
| 200M QAT sensitivity | **0.5B QAT** per finalist from a completed 2.5B dense parent, plus a matched dense continuation | Conversion and short adaptation signal; no claim about final long QAT. |
| Width bridge at 1536 and 2304 | **0.5B diagnostic checkpoint**, then **2.5B dense** per transferred LR/control being compared at each width | Holds horizon, data, batch, and depth fixed to test width transfer. Do not select from the diagnostic checkpoint alone. |
| Serious 1.5B geometry/recipe confirmation | **2B diagnostic checkpoint** of a **10B dense** schedule; complete **10B dense**, then **2B QAT** for finalists and a matched dense continuation | Tests a longer target-width learning curve and ternary adaptation. One 10B+2B result is still only a pilot relative to a trillion-token production horizon. |
| Duration bridge if the 1.5T-token production aim remains | Separate fully decayed **2.5B, 10B, and conditionally 30B** runs at small width, with at least a local LR bracket; check a narrow bracket at 1.5B | Estimates how rate ranking shifts with horizon. Requires fresh multilingual data and explicit funding; even this does not prove the 1.5T optimum. |

The first geometry queue costs **6B dense tokens** for twelve screens. Continuing at least one LR per depth adds **6B**, for **12B dense minimum**. Probing the top two adds **1B QAT plus 1B same-data dense continuation**. Additional HPO totals depend on which rows survive their budget gates; the trial cap and GPU-hour cap are priced from the selected queue.

No credible fixed price exists until stack throughput and memory are measured at all three widths with the actual batch and QAT mode. The 1.5B stack measured **39,006 tokens/s** in a synthetic whole-step H100 sweep (receipt summarized in `trial/stack/004` commit `19f9ad2`); the earlier non-stack reference measured 46,395 tokens/s. The stage-one 200M stack measured 143.5K tokens/s and 86.9 GiB at its full stage. At 39,006 tokens/s, **2.5B / 10B / 2B QAT-equivalent tokens** take approximately **17.8 / 71.2 / 14.2 active H100 hours** respectively, before real-data validation/checkpoint/startup overhead; QAT may have a different rate. One 10B-dense-plus-2B-QAT finalist is therefore at least roughly **85 active H100 hours** at that rate. A 50–60-hour cap can support a **pilot**, not a convincing target-width search with repeats and QAT confirmation. The 1.5T-token aspiration in the separate feasibility study is a production-scale target, not the exposure of these research trials.

For every proposed trial, price `Σ_phase(tokens / measured all-in tokens_per_second × GPU_count / 3600) + measured startup/archive overhead`, reserve recovery capacity, and reject jobs whose worst credible cost exceeds the remaining cap. The all-in rate includes validation and checkpoint pauses; never count those twice. The owner selects the paid cap after stage-0 throughput receipts; the harness locks it before launch. Present a pilot option (numerical gate, proxy LR/batch region, one width bridge, short QAT diagnostics) and a separately funded confirmation option (multi-seed multilingual target-width dense plus full QAT). The plan intentionally supplies no cloud-launch command or implied authorization.

**Ready for owner review when:** `hpo/v1` manifests and gates are implemented and locally tested; exact architecture/parameterization/score/data versions and proposed spend are readable; native-GPU checks still required are listed; and the first trial queue, estimated cost, and stop rules are concrete. After owner launch, keep one committed experiment per verified Azure cycle and stop at the cap or when no meaningful comparison fits.

## 9. Build milestones and deliverables

1. **Kernel milestone:** profile the selected 1.5B stack, isolate and benchmark each acceleration against the tuned baseline, verify the retained implementation on one H100 at proxy/bridge/target widths and dense/QAT modes, and freeze its source/runtime hashes. The existing combined fusion prototype is slower; [K0–K5](PRE_HPO_KERNEL_GATE.md) specify its diagnosis and fallback. Native checks and any GPU spend remain owner-launched. Reprice HPO after this gate.
2. **Specification milestone:** checked architecture-family/role table, base/delta shape metadata, mathematical µP/Muon note, a target-geometry decision or `geometry/v1` proposal, versioned HPO policy, trial schema, protected scorer, fixed eval/task manifest, and first costed trial queue. Review the readout and all logical Muon slices against both paper equations and code.
3. **Local harness milestone:** implement recipe resolution, policy hashing, immutable trial IDs, append-only ledger, score vector/status, cost estimator, and owner-launch handoff. CPU tests cover invalid manifests, exact optimizer group coverage, fused-slice updates, schedule endpoints, batch arithmetic, checkpoint/transition mapping, and lock drift. Push this separately from optimizer math changes.
4. **Native HPO gate milestone:** owner-launched short H100 validation with SM90 parity for HPO optimizer and parameterization choices, real-data short resume, and verified archive/deallocation receipts. Reuse the kernel gate's all-in rates only when runtime/batch/optimizer/mode hashes match. Fail this gate closed; do not infer native quality from CPU tests.
5. **Search milestone:** lock the paid campaign after pricing, execute one committed recipe per cycle, review all diagnostic channels, and maintain a live Pareto frontier. Stage reports state remaining cap and what can still be compared meaningfully.
6. **Confirmation milestone:** target-width multilingual dense and ternary runs, paired dense-continuation controls, repeated close recipes, final untouched evaluation, and a production recipe with exact commit/config/manifest/checkpoint hashes plus remaining uncertainties.

## 10. Optional research branch, contingent on evidence

Muon++ ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01306), §4) proposes maintaining spectral conditions by controlling updates. `µnit Scaling` ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05967)) proposes a different FP8-friendly parameterization. Both could help if our coordinate/spectral or FP8 gates fail, but each changes optimizer/numerical semantics and would require its own paired campaign version and native tests. Neither should be inserted into the first tuning loop merely because it is new. The first genuinely useful addition is the **telescoping width calibration with sentinel points**, because it directly addresses the observed gap between a cheap 200M proxy and a 1.5B target while keeping uncertainty visible.
